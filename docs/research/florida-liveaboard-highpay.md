### Fort Lauderdale day-work strategy

**Best timing**
- Arrive in **September–October**, before the Fort Lauderdale International Boat Show and Caribbean-season departure push.
- Dock-walk early, **7:30–9:30 AM**, or late afternoon, **5:00–6:00 PM**.
- Avoid lunchtime and mid-day when crews are busiest.
- Day work commonly pays about **$150–$250/day** for washdowns, detailing, provisioning, and yard support.

**What to bring**
- 10–20 printed CVs with a professional headshot
- STCW Basic Training certificate copy
- Reference list
- Phone with photos of your work
- Clean polo, khakis or clean jeans, and non-marking deck shoes

**How to approach boats**
- Ask for the **deckhand, bosun, first mate, or chief stew** first; they can direct you to the captain.
- Keep it short: “Hi, I’m a local deckhand with 1,000+ hours underway and STCW Basic Training. I’m available today or this week for day work—do you need an extra hand?”
- If they say “not today,” ask when to check back and return every few days.
- Follow up with crew agencies after dock-walking.

**Best Fort Lauderdale dock-walking spots**
- **Bahia Mar Yachting Center / 17th Street corridor** — major crew and yacht hub
- **Las Olas Marina** — large megayacht marina and boat-show center
- **Sunrise Harbor Marina** — high-end yachts, walkable crew area
- **New River Downtown Docks / Riverwalk** — central yacht and crew area
- **Fort Lauderdale Marina**
- **Hall of Fame Marina**
- **Pier Sixty-Six Marina**
- **New River / Intracoastal yacht yards**

**Biggest opportunity:** The **Fort Lauderdale International Boat Show (FLIBS)**. Many yachts need extra hands before, during, and after the show for prep, detailing, provisioning, and crew support.

## ⚓ Part B — Entry-Level U.S. Maritime Jobs: $24+/hr or $50k+

### Credential reality check

- **MMC (Merchant Mariner Credential):** Required for many commercial vessel roles, especially OS/AB, tug, offshore, research, and government positions.
- **TWIC (Transportation Worker Identification Credential):** Required by most port-access employers; often needed before orientation or boarding.
- **Green deckhand jobs without MMC first:** More common in inland towing, some ferry systems, and certain dredging or workboat roles.
- **Apply early:** Even where MMC is not required at application, having it “in progress” makes you much more competitive.

## 🛂 Part C — MMC and TWIC: Do This First

| Credential | What it is | Why it matters | How to get it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MMC** | U.S. Coast Guard Merchant Mariner Credential | Required for many commercial vessel jobs; entry endorsement is usually **Ordinary Seaman (OS)** or **Wiper** | Apply through the USCG National Maritime Center; submit application, ID, medical certificate, drug-test documentation, and sea-service evidence |
| **TWIC** | TSA Transportation Worker Identification Credential | Required for secure port, terminal, and vessel access | Apply through TSA/TWIC enrollment; background check and fingerprinting required |
| **STCW Basic Training** | Safety training required for many commercial and yacht roles | You already have this—include it prominently on your CV | Keep the original certificate and carry copies |
| **Sea service** | Documented time underway | Your 1,000+ hours are valuable; make them easy for employers and the USCG to verify | Keep a sea-service form or logbook signed by captains/owners |

**Recommended application order**
1. Start **TWIC** immediately.
2. Start **MMC with OS endorsement** immediately.
3. Apply to **Magnolia Marine, Ingram, Canal Barge, Marquette, Campbell, and Crounse** for green deckhand roles.
4. Register with **TCN, Crewfinders, All Ocean Yachts, Yotspot, Crew4Yachts, and Crewbay**.
5. Dock-walk Fort Lauderdale daily during the fall hiring window.
6. Apply to **Washington State Ferries, NYC Ferry, Casco Bay Lines, and Block Island Ferry** for commercial sea time.
7. Once MMC is issued, apply to **NOAA, Scripps, UNOLS, MSC, Edison Chouest, Crowley, Moran, and dredging companies**.
